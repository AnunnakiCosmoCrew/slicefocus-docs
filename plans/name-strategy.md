# Getting the name known: SliceFocus, with CosmoCrew and Luvita behind it

*Written: 2026-11-06 | Status: the order of the three names was agreed by the founder on 2026-11-06 (SliceFocus first); the signature was approved the same day; the picture and the app changes are still open | Founder check: public (it sets what every public surface says) and product (two of the steps are changes to the app)*

This is the companion to [growth-strategy.md](growth-strategy.md). That document says how we reach 20 weekly users. This one says how people come to know the name at all. It has no dates; the work is ordered by what depends on what.

## The answer in short

A name becomes known when the right people meet the same few things again and again, at the moments they need this kind of app, and increasingly from other people instead of from us.

So we do four things, in this order:

1. **Fix one signature**: one name, one picture, one sentence, used everywhere without variation.
2. **Tie the name to a handful of moments of need**, on the pages that people, search engines and AI assistants read.
3. **Let the app carry its own name**, by making what people do in it easy to show to others.
4. **Borrow other people's voices one person at a time**, and keep the big one-off launches for when the app holds on to its users.

## Three names, three jobs

| Name | Its job | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SliceFocus** | The name people search for and say. It leads everywhere | Every listing, page, picture and post |
| **CosmoCrew** | The maker's signature on the consumer apps, so the next app (LexiPower, With Handlebars) starts with some trust | A small, constant "by CosmoCrew" under the product name |
| **Luvita** | The company, for partners and reviewers | The footer line every surface already carries (luvita-docs ADR 0001). Never LinkedIn |

One name gets the repetition. Splitting a small amount of attention across three names would leave none of them known (reasons 1 and 5).

## The reasoning

### 1. A name is learned by repetition over time, not by one big moment.

People come to prefer and recall what they have met several times. The effect is modest for each meeting and adds up.

What backs it:
- A review of about 200 experiments found a reliable, moderate effect of repeated exposure on liking ([Bornstein, *Psychological Bulletin*, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.106.2.265)). Mostly laboratory studies.
- A large body of advertising case data finds that brand effects build slowly, over six months and more, and outlast short bursts (Binet and Field, *The Long and the Short of It*, IPA, 2013). Industry research on big advertisers, read here as a direction.

What follows: start now, keep it small and steady, and judge it over months. It also follows that name work is not a substitute for the growth strategy's first stage. It runs beside it.

### 2. People recognise one thing, so we show the same thing every time.

Recognition comes from a few fixed signs repeated without change: a name, a shape, a line. We already have a rare one. Almost no other planner looks like a 24-hour clock.

What backs it:
- The Ehrenberg-Bass Institute's work on "distinctive brand assets" (Sharp, *How Brands Grow*, 2010; Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets*, 2018). Widely used marketing research, built on large consumer brands.
- Our own experience: the June post that opened with the clock in motion was viewed about 6,500 times in a community of 77,000. One case, not a test.

### 3. A known name only helps if it comes to mind at the moment of need.

Nobody wakes up wanting "SliceFocus". They want to plan a study day, or to stop drifting to a website while they work. The name has to be attached to those moments, in the places where people ask.

What backs it:
- The same research calls these moments "category entry points" and treats links to them as what makes a brand come to mind (Romaniuk, *Better Brand Health*, 2023).
- Apple says about 65 % of App Store downloads follow a search ([Apple Ads](https://ads.apple.com/app-store)), and that search reads the name, subtitle, keywords and category ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)).
- Our own data: AI assistants are already one of the larger sources of visits to slicefocus.app, ahead of ordinary web search (figures on the metrics dashboard). People now ask an assistant "which app for this?", and the assistant answers from pages that connect a name to a need.

### 4. Other people saying the name counts for more than us saying it, and they say it when they see it.

What backs it:
- Across more than 300 products, the ones that are publicly visible, or that everyday surroundings remind people of, were talked about more, both at once and months later. Being merely interesting helped only at first ([Berger and Schwartz, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2011](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/BzzAgent.pdf)).
- Mentions by ordinary people each move less than a press article, but there are far more of them, so in total they moved sales more, and they made press coverage more likely ([Stephen and Galak, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2012](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2012/november/nov9_socialmedia.html)). One marketplace.
- Useful content and content that stirs people is shared more ([Berger and Milkman, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353)). News articles, not apps.
- Customers who arrive by word of mouth were worth about twice as much over time as those bought through marketing ([Villanueva, Yoo and Hanssens, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2008](https://www.msi.org/working-papers/the-impact-of-marketinginduced-versus-wordofmouth-customer-acquisition-on-customer-equity/)). One company.

What follows: today the app is used in private, so nobody sees it being used. The most valuable name work is making use visible.

### 5. One name at a time; the maker's name rides along.

A product name that is endorsed by a maker's name keeps its own identity and lends the maker some of its reputation ([Aaker and Joachimsthaler, *California Management Review*, 2000](https://cmr-mig.berkeley.edu/2000/08/42-4-the-brand-relationship-spectrum-the-key-to-the-brand-architecture-challenge)). An established way of arranging brands, not an experiment.

What follows: SliceFocus leads. "by CosmoCrew" travels with it, always in the same small form. When the next app is promoted, the signature is already familiar and the same steps repeat.

### 6. Put the name where it stays, and where nobody can take it down.

A forum post is seen for a day and can be removed, as Reddit showed on 3 November. A listing, a page, a video or an article keeps showing the name for years.

What backs it: our own experience, and reason 1. Lasting places repeat the name without anyone having to repeat the work.

## The way

### A. Fix the signature (once)

- **One spelling:** SliceFocus. Under it, small: by CosmoCrew.
- **One picture:** the 24-hour clock with a day drawn on it. The same day, the same colours, wherever the app is shown.
- **One sentence:** what the app is, in the words users themselves use. Until the watched sessions give us those words, the line the website already opens with: "Plan your day on a clock. It runs itself." (See "Step A: where we stand" below.)
- Put the three on every surface: the App Store listing, the website, directories, GitHub, every profile and every draft. Keep them on one page in this repository so that nothing drifts.

**Why first:** reason 2. Everything after this repeats the signature, so it must be settled before it is repeated.

### B. Own five moments of need

The five to start with (our judgement, to be corrected by what users say):

1. Planning a study day.
2. Time-blocking a work day.
3. A Pomodoro timer that runs itself.
4. Keeping distracting apps and sites away while working.
5. Seeing where the day actually went.

For each one: the words in the App Store listing, one plain page on slicefocus.app that answers it, the comparison pages, and a directory entry. Written to answer the question, with no tricks aimed at AI assistants (the October research found those do not hold up).

**Why second:** reasons 3 and 6. These pages are what search engines and assistants read, and they take months to be found, so they start early.

### C. Let the app carry its own name (changes to the app)

- **A picture of my day to share:** one tap produces the day's clock as an image, with the name on it. Students and planners already post their schedules; today ours leaves no trace.
- **Ready-made days as pages:** the app already ships day templates (Student Day, Deep Work Sprint, Maker Schedule and others). Each becomes a page on the site that opens in the app, so a useful thing carries the name.
- **Ratings:** the prompt after the third finished session is already in place.

**Why third:** reason 4. It needs development time, and it pays most once regular users exist to do the showing.

### D. Borrow voices, one person at a time

- People who already teach planning, studying or focus: makers of study and productivity videos, newsletter writers, university study-skills pages, writers who cover iPhone and Mac apps, and Apple's own editors (a featuring nomination before each release).
- A personal note from the founder with the picture and the sentence, to one person at a time. No mass mailing, no payment.
- Some of these people can also be among the fifteen hand-picked users of the growth strategy.

**Why fourth:** reason 4, and each mention is a lasting place (reason 6). The share that will answer is unknown.

### E. Keep the big one-off moments for later

Show HN, Product Hunt and a return to Reddit stay behind the growth strategy's condition: people who try the app keep using it.

### F. Repeat for the next app

LexiPower and With Handlebars follow the same steps, with "by CosmoCrew" already familiar.

## How we will know the name is getting out

- **People who arrive already knowing it:** direct visits to slicefocus.app and searches for the name.
- **Places that say the name that we did not write:** a list kept by hand, plus where the site's visitors come from, AI assistants included.
- **How often the App Store listing is seen.**
- In the end, installs and weekly users. Those belong to the growth strategy.

## What we are not doing, and why

- **Paying for reach now:** the founder's decision of 6 November, and the growth strategy's reason 5.
- **Being on many networks at once:** reason 1. Thin presence everywhere repeats nothing.
- **LinkedIn, a new Reddit account:** standing rules.
- **Tricks aimed at AI assistants:** they did not hold up when re-tested. Plain pages that answer real questions are the whole method.

## Where the evidence is thin

- The brand research (reasons 2 and 3) was built on large consumer brands, not on a free app with a handful of users.
- The repetition studies are mostly laboratory work.
- The word-of-mouth studies are about other kinds of product.
- How assistants choose what to cite is not well understood. We only know they already send us visitors.
- The five moments, the share-a-picture idea, the template pages and the share of people who will answer a personal note are our judgement.
