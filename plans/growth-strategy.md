# SliceFocus growth strategy: the way to 20 weekly users

*Written: 2026-11-06 | Status: proposed, for the founder's decision | Founder check: product (it changes where the founder's time goes) and public (every public text stays a draft the founder approves and posts)*

This document has no dates on purpose. Each stage starts when its condition is met. Its companion, [name-strategy.md](name-strategy.md), says how people come to know the name. The dated record of what was done and when is [promotion-plan-2026-10-29.md](promotion-plan-2026-10-29.md).

## The answer in short

Twenty weekly users is a small number, so we reach it by hand first and by broadcast later.

1. First we make sure a handful of the right people keep using the app. We bring them in ourselves and watch what happens.
2. Alongside, we make the app easy to find in the places we control and the places Apple runs.
3. Once people are staying, we ask communities for attention.
4. Only after that do we spend money.

Each step makes the next one worth more. Done in the other order, each step wastes the one before it.

## Where we stand

- 1.7.4 is live on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and it starts without an account.
- Few people find the App Store listing, and few of them install. Of the people who installed before 1.7.4, very few came back. We do not know yet whether 1.7.4 changed that. (The figures are on the metrics dashboard; this repository is public.)
- Reddit is paused after the account ban of 3 November.
- Founder's decisions so far: organic before paid, and people should use the app on both iPhone and Mac.

## The reasoning

### 1. Keeping people multiplies everything. Reach only adds.

The number of weekly users we hold is roughly: installs a week × the share who become regulars × how many weeks a regular stays.

A worked example, assuming a regular stays ten weeks (an assumption, to show the shape):

| Share of new users who become regulars | Installs a week needed to hold 20 weekly users |
| --- | --- |
| 3 in 100 (about where the old versions were) | 67 |
| 10 in 100 | 20 |
| 25 in 100 | 8 |
| 40 in 100 | 5 |

If people stay, something close to today's trickle of installs is enough. If they do not, we need many times today's reach, every week, for ever. So the first question is whether people stay, and reach is the second.

What backs it:
- A study of five companies found that improving retention by 1 % raised the value of the customer base by about 5 %, against about 0.1 % for the same improvement in the cost of winning a customer ([Gupta, Lehmann and Stuart, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2004](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/534/Valuing_Customers--JMR_2003.pdf)). Those were paid services, so the size will differ for a free app. The direction is the point.
- The table is our own arithmetic, not a forecast.

### 2. The goal is small, so the first users should be found by hand.

Reddit, Hacker News and ads are tools for reaching thousands. Our goal is twenty. For a number that small, the proportionate method is personal: find people with the problem, put the app in their hands, and watch.

It also answers the question we cannot answer from numbers. About five people trying a product in front of you surface most of its usability problems. And fifteen people are enough to tell "3 in 100 stay" from "30 in 100 stay", which is the difference we care about. They are not enough to tell 30 from 40, and we do not need that yet.

What backs it:
- Usability testing with about five people finds most problems ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/), after Nielsen and Landauer, 1993). Established practice.
- Recruiting the first users one by one is standard advice for a product at this stage ([Paul Graham, "Do Things that Don't Scale"](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)). A widely followed essay, not a study.
- The statistics: 5 of 15 staying gives a 95 % range of roughly 15 to 58 in 100, well clear of the old rate.

The weak point: people we know are polite. So we choose them by the problem they have, not by friendship, and we judge by what they do in the app in their third week, not by what they say.

### 3. Build on ground we own. Treat borrowed audiences as a bonus.

One automatic filter ended the Reddit plan in a day, with no warning and no appeal answered. Any community we do not run can do the same. The App Store listing and the website cannot be taken away, and work on them keeps paying back.

What backs it:
- Apple says about 65 % of App Store downloads follow a search ([Apple Ads](https://ads.apple.com/app-store)). Apple's own figure, with no method published.
- Apple says search results depend on the words in the name, subtitle, keywords and category, and on downloads, ratings and reviews ([Apple, "Discovery on the App Store"](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)). Apple gives the factors and not their weights.
- Ratings move app downloads in a study of 60 countries ([Kübler, Pauwels, Yildirim and Fandrich, *Journal of Marketing*, 2018](https://scinapse.io/papers/2808333075)).

### 4. Save the big chances for when the app is ready for them.

A post that takes off brings one burst of people, once. Our own count from Hacker News search: of 98 "Show HN" posts about Pomodoro apps since January 2025, half got 2 points or fewer and about 5 in 100 reached 10. So a hit is rare. If it comes while new users still leave on day one, it is spent for nothing, and Hacker News buries a second posting of something that already had attention ([HN FAQ](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsfaq.html)).

So the earlier plans were right to hold Show HN back, but the condition was the wrong one. It should be "people stay", which fifteen hand-picked users can show, and not "100 installs", which we have no cheap way to get.

### 5. Money amplifies what works. It cannot find it.

An ad buys visits to whatever we already have. Before people stay, it buys visits to a leak. Before we know which words bring the right people, it buys the wrong visits.

What backs it:
- In one long-run study, customers who came through word of mouth were worth about twice as much over time as customers bought through marketing, whose effect faded within weeks ([Villanueva, Yoo and Hanssens, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2008](https://www.msi.org/working-papers/the-impact-of-marketinginduced-versus-wordofmouth-customer-acquisition-on-customer-equity/)). One company, a web host, so treat it as a direction.
- This matches the founder's own decision of 6 November: organic first.

## The way

### Stage 1: make sure people stay, by hand

- Bring in about fifteen people who already plan their day or use a focus timer. Two groups to try, because we do not know yet who the app is really for: students with long self-directed study days, and people who work on a Mac and block out their time.
- Watch the first five minutes of at least five of them without helping. Fix what three of five trip over.
- Ask each one, after a week, what the app is for. Their words become the words of the listing and the site.
- Look at what they do in the third week.

**Why first:** reasons 1 and 2. Everything after this stage is worth more once it is done.

**Move on when:** at least five of the fifteen still plan and run blocks in their third week, and we can say who the app is for in one sentence a user would say. If fewer stay, the problem is in the product, and the next work is there, not in promotion.

### Stage 2: be easy to find and easy to say yes to (runs alongside stage 1)

- The App Store listing: the words in the subtitle and keywords, revisited at every release with what stage 1 taught us.
- The first screenshots say what the app is at a glance.
- Ratings: the in-app prompt after the third finished session is already in place. Ratings come from regulars, so this grows with stage 1.
- Apple's free placements: a featuring nomination before every release, and an In-App Event when a release brings something big.
- The website: the comparison pages and the "no sign-up" line, kept current.
- Directories where people look for alternatives.

**Why alongside:** reason 3. It costs little, nobody can take it away, and it takes months to build up, so it should start now.

**Healthy when:** release over release, more people see the listing and a larger share of them install.

### Stage 3: ask communities for attention

- One community at a time, starting where the founder is already a member or can become one honestly. Turkish communities belong here, and so does Reddit if the account comes back.
- Read the rules first. Tell a story, say you made it, use one plain link.
- Show HN belongs here, told as how the app is built.

**Why after stage 1:** reason 4.

**Start when:** stage 1's condition is met.

### Stage 4: spend money to amplify

- A small, capped test on App Store search first, because people searching there already want this kind of app.
- Read the people who came through ads separately from everyone else.

**Why last:** reason 5.

**Start when:** stage 1's condition is met, we know which words bring people who stay, and the founder has set a cap.

## Rules we keep throughout

- The founder posts everything. Claude drafts and never posts.
- Say you are the maker in the first line. One plain link.
- Never suggest the app treats ADHD.
- Never LinkedIn. No new Reddit account.
- Small numbers are read as ranges, not as single figures.
- Before every release: nominate it for featuring at least three weeks ahead, and revisit the listing's words.

## How we will know

- **The one number:** weekly users who run three blocks.
- **Early signs that people stay:** the share of new users who finish a first session, and the share still active in their third week.
- **Signs that people find us:** how often the listing is seen, and the share who install.

## What we are not doing, and why

- **Paid ads now:** reason 5, and the founder's decision.
- **A big launch now** (Show HN, Product Hunt): reason 4.
- **A screenshot A/B test:** the listing has too few visitors for a test to reach an answer.
- **Emailing existing users:** they have not agreed to marketing email.
- **Short-video channels and Setapp:** a steady cost in the founder's time for an unproven return; Setapp pays nothing for a free app.

## Where the evidence is thin

- The studies behind reasons 1 and 5 are about paid services and one web host, not free apps.
- Nobody has studied starting without an account for an app like this one.
- Apple names what affects search and does not say how much.
- Our own numbers are tiny.
- The thresholds (fifteen people, five still active in week three) are our judgement, not research.

## What this changes from the 6 November plan

- **Stays:** the five watched sessions, the In-App Event, the directory listings, the website work. They are stages 1 and 2.
- **Moves later:** Show HN and the Turkish forum post, from "the week of 16 November" to "when stage 1's condition is met".
- **New:** bringing in about fifteen people by hand, and a clear condition for when promotion starts.
